# 2. UNDERSTAND THE CUSTOMER QUESTIONS

The V1 platform is expected to help customers answer these questions:

### 1. Which cells are showing thermal anomalies?

Determine what the system would need to:

* identify a thermal anomaly
* locate the affected cell/module
* determine severity
* provide evidence
* distinguish normal thermal variation from an actual anomaly
* indicate confidence
* show historical context

---

### 2. Which vehicle is likely to overheat?

Determine what information is required to make a vehicle-level overheating assessment.

Consider:

* Current thermal state
* Temperature trends
* Maximum temperature
* Rate of temperature rise
* Battery operating conditions
* Cooling performance
* Historical thermal behavior
* Battery health/aging effects
* Thermal exposure
* Current operating conditions

Do not assume the final prediction methodology.

Determine what the problem actually requires.

---

### 3. Is the cooling system behaving correctly?

Determine what "correct cooling behavior" means from a system perspective.

Identify the information that may be required, such as:

* coolant temperatures
* cooling commands
* flow
* inlet/outlet behavior
* thermal response
* expected versus observed temperature reduction
* ambient conditions
* battery load
* thermal lag

Determine how the platform could conceptually identify:

```text
Normal Cooling
        vs
Degraded Cooling
        vs
Cooling Fault
        vs
Uncertain
```

Do not implement this yet.

---

### 4. Is a temperature sensor faulty?

Analyze the requirement for:

* sensor bias
* drift
* stuck-at values
* dropout
* noise
* time shift
* inconsistent neighboring temperatures
* model residuals
* disagreement between physical and virtual measurements

Determine what evidence is required before declaring a sensor faulty.

Also distinguish:

```text
Sensor Fault
vs
Real Thermal Event
vs
Cooling Problem
vs
Data/Communication Problem
```

---

### 5. Which batteries are aging faster because of thermal exposure?

Understand this requirement carefully.

Determine what "thermal exposure" could mean, such as:

* sustained high temperature
* repeated thermal peaks
* high temperature duration
* thermal cycling
* temperature gradients
* hotspot exposure
* cooling degradation
* operating-condition interaction

Determine what the system would need to calculate or track to support a defensible thermal-aging assessment.

Do not assume that we are directly predicting SOH unless the available requirements support it.

---

### 6. Do I need to derate charging or power?

This is a particularly important safety-boundary requirement.

Determine what the platform would need to provide to support a **derating recommendation**.

Consider:

* maximum temperature
* temperature rise rate
* hotspot probability
* cooling state
* uncertainty
* battery condition
* operating mode
* thermal risk level
* forecasted thermal trajectory

However, maintain the handbook's safety boundary.

The analytics platform must not autonomously control a production BMS or safety actuator unless that requirement is explicitly established later.

Clearly distinguish:

```text
Analytics / Advisory Recommendation
vs
Actual BMS Control
```

---

Determine how thermal-management evidence could contribute to maintenance prioritization.

Potential evidence may include:

* recurring thermal anomalies
* repeated overheating
* sensor faults
* cooling-system degradation
* abnormal temperature gradients
* thermal exposure history
* increasing thermal residual
* abnormal thermal response

Determine what should constitute:

```text
Monitor
Maintenance Recommended
High Priority
```

Analyze the requirement carefully.

Determine how the platform would need to answer:

```text

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
Prediction accuracy
        ↓
Hotspot coverage
        ↓

        ↓
Uncertainty
        ↓
```

The system must not recommend sensor reduction based only on average prediction error.

Consider:

* hotspot recall

* confidence
* sensor fault isolation
* spatial coverage
* operating conditions
* failure/fallback behavior

This must remain consistent with the safety limitations defined in the handbook.
